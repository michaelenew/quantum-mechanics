## R6 — Lorentzian real time ✅

The theorem (RP ⟹ OS ⟹ Hilbert space and unitary time) has stood for
a long while with nothing ever evolved. The constrained kernel already
contains the answer.

**Dispersion.** Continue Euclidean k₀ → iE. The subtlety that has to
be got right: `d` and its partner continue **independently** —
`d₀ = e^{−E}−1` and `e^{+E}−1`, *not* complex conjugates — because
only then does their product give the continued `−4sinh²(E/2)`. A
first pass used the conjugate and found no pole at all.

| \|k\| | E | E/\|k\| |
|---|---|---|
| 0.13090 | 0.130713 | **0.99858** |
| 0.52360 | 0.512026 | 0.97790 |
| 1.04720 | 0.962424 | 0.91905 |

Against the closed form `E = 2 arcsinh(k̂/2)`: **max relative
difference 6.66e−16**. Speed → 1 as k → 0; the deviation is lattice
dispersion, O(a²).

**Polarisations.** The transverse-traceless subspace is

## R7 — matter content: spinors ✅

Bar row (E) has said "no spinors" since 0127. It closes, and for the
same structural reason that decided item 6.

**A spinor cannot be parallel-transported by a metric.** It needs an
element of the spin group. And this lattice already has one: Spin(4) =
SU(2)⁺ × SU(2)⁻ *is* the spin group, and the link variables are its
elements.

| check | result |
|---|---|
| Clifford algebra {γ^a,γ^b} = 2δ^{ab} | **0.00e+00** |
| the plaquettes' SO(4) rotation = the square of the spinor rep | **2.22e−16** |
| intertwiner `SγᵃS† = Λᵃ_b γᵇ` | **1.57e−16** |

**The covariance test, and the instructive way it fails first:**

| | spectrum moves by |
|---|---|
| links rotated, tetrad left flat | **3.31e−02** |
| links **and tetrad** rotated | **9.33e−15** |

That contrast *is* R7's point. Rotating the connection alone breaks
the Dirac operator; rotating it together with the **tetrad** leaves it
exactly invariant. The tetrad is what makes a spinor index and a
spacetime index commensurable — and only the constrained sector has
one. The induced formulation's sole variable is `W = √g g^{μν}`, a
symmetric tensor with no action on a spinor index at all.

Free spectrum on 8⁴: **16 exact zero modes** — the naive
discretisation's 2⁴ doublers. Nielsen–Ninomiya applies here as
anywhere; Wilson or overlap removes them and is orthogonal to anything
this program claims.

## R5 — nonlinearity ⛔ **obstructed at the extraction**

**What is built and gated.** The Palatini action is **exactly
quartic** in (h, ω) — e·e is quadratic in e, F is quadratic in ω, and
the expansion *terminates*. There is no truncation anywhere.

| gate | result |
|---|---|
| ∂S/∂h analytic vs numerical | 4.31e−08 |
| ∂S/∂ω analytic vs numerical | 1.95e−07 |
| momentum-space operator vs position-space gradient | **1.000000 / 1.000000**, scatter 3.7e−10 |
| second-order source scales as s² | 0.15976 / 0.15968 / 0.15952 |

Getting there took three corrections worth recording: gradients
convert differently from fields (off-diagonal pairs pick up both index
orders); the "linearised" gradient must be extracted by amplitude
scaling, not by hand-picking terms; and the ω-gradient carries a
factor 2 on the B block that the A block does not (bilinear vs
quadratic).

**What does not work.** β does not converge:

    h1_00 = -3.48537e-03/r + 4.17e-04            (residual 8.0e-03)
    h2_00 = +8.46e-06/r (fixed by the source monopole)
            -4.24e-06/r^2 + ...                  (residual 7.1e-02)
    beta = -0.698     (GR: +1)

Palatini gives β = 1 as a **theorem**, so −0.70 is an extraction
artifact and is not reported as a measurement. Two named causes:

1. **Conditioning.** The usable window is r = 1…6 on a 24³ box.
   Separating a 1/r from a 1/r² over a factor 6 in r, on a periodic
   lattice with a zero-mode constant, is badly conditioned — h₁'s own
   fit already carries 8e−3.
2. **Second-order gauge.** γ is gauge-insensitive at first order; **β
   is not.** A de Donder term was applied at both orders without
   establishing that it corresponds to the gauge in which β = 1 is
   defined. This is the more likely culprit and it is not a
   conditioning problem.

**What would fix it:** a substantially larger box so r spans a decade,
and an explicit second-order gauge transformation to the standard PPN
form. Both are ordinary work; neither is a new idea.

Recorded honestly: one real physical finding of the second-order
solve did survive — the source has a nonzero monopole, i.e. the
**gravitational binding energy renormalises the mass**, measured at
−0.002 × GM. That is a genuine nonlinear effect and it is why a pure
1/r² fit to h₂ drifts.
