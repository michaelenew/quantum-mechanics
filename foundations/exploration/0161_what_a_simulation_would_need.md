(double-copy / simplicity) sector, not the induced-matter one: the
induced kernel violates diffeomorphism invariance by 1.7e15 relative
to Einstein-Hilbert, and the violation is *flat in k* (exponent
+0.029) so it does not vanish in the continuum (0160).

would actually drive a simulation — were built and measured in the
**induced** sector:

  the background weight `W = √g g^{μν}`;
- item 4's 1/r (ratio 1.0722, spread 1.68% over ×15 in r) is the
  response of *that* action.

> Both are real measurements of a real quantity — the induced scale
> stiffness — and neither is the gravitational source or the
> gravitational response. **The two items a simulation most needs were
> done in the sector item 6 then ruled out.**

## So what can "a GR simulation from the continuous quantum theory"
## honestly mean?

The composition, not a direct run:

    derived weight  →  balanced reps = simplicity (0160 s3)
                    →  constrained sector
                    →  local + 2-derivative + diffeo-invariant
                    →  UNIQUELY Einstein-Hilbert (0160 s1)
                    →  the classical tier's integrator (0037)

Each arrow is measured or forced, not assumed. The last stage — the
thing that actually produces orbits, bending and precession — runs at
the **classical** tier, and already does: bending 0.008046 against
0.008000, precession converging to 6πM/p, two-body nonlinearity
localised at O(M₁M₂).

That is not a dodge, but it is a real limitation and should be stated
as one: **nobody simulates QCD to compute a planetary orbit either.**
The scale separation that makes the direct simulation impossible is
the same scale separation that makes the effective description
correct. What the program can claim is the *derivation chain*, and
what it cannot claim is a lattice that shows you a geodesic.
