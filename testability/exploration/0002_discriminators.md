# 0002 — Experimental discriminators

For a theory to be "testable" we must say what would confirm it, what would be
consistent-but-not-decisive, and what would break it. Each entry states the
prediction and, crucially, whether it distinguishes this theory from standard QM
or merely from a strawman.

## Tier 1 — Decisive against Branch B (already resolved)

**D1. Loophole-free Bell test.**
- Branch A (interpretation): `|S| ≤ 2√2`, violation expected. ✓ consistent.
- Branch B (classical knowledge): `|S| ≤ 2`, no violation.
- Verdict: experiment shows violation (2015, loophole-free) → **Branch B
  falsified.** This is the single most important test and it is already done.

## Tier 2 — Distinctive candidate (the place to look for real daylight)

**D2. Quantum-reference-frame dependence of superposition / entanglement.**
Take literally "the wavefunction is B's state in A's own frame." Giacomini et
al. (2019) show that whether a system is in superposition — even whether two
systems are entangled — depends on the reference frame, and transforms
covariantly under a QRF change. Proposals exist to probe this with relational
observables (e.g. interference visibility defined relative to an internal frame).
- Prediction of this theory: superposition/entanglement are **frame-relative**;
  the "collapse" one perspective reports need not be the collapse another reports
  until they coincide (P5).
- Status: QRF is standard QM re-expressed, so this is *not* a deviation from QM —
  but it is a deviation from any interpretation that insists on a single absolute
  fact of collapse. It discriminates *among interpretations*, and some of its
  relational predictions are experimentally accessible. **Best lead.**

## Tier 3 — Consistency checks (support, not discrimination)

**D3. SPDC / EPR relative coordinates.**
Split events should produce sharp relative position `q` and sharp total momentum
`P`, with the *relative* position–momentum inference product below the
single-particle Heisenberg bound (Reid/EPR criterion).
- Prediction: `Δ_inf(q)·Δ_inf(p_rel) < ħ/2` achievable across the pair while
  each particle alone obeys `Δq·Δp ≥ ħ/2`.
- Status: **observed** (Howell et al. 2004). Confirms the split/merge mechanism;
  does not distinguish from standard QM (it *is* standard QM). Supportive.

**D4. Large-molecule interferometry (buckyball-class).**
- Prediction: internal relative coordinates stay sharp; only CoM which-path
  coherence is lost to the environment; molecules arrive intact; interference
  visibility falls with controllable environmental monitoring of CoM.
- Status: matches matter-wave interferometry results. Supportive, not decisive.

**D5. "Could-have-known" (in-principle) which-path erasure.**
- Prediction: interference is destroyed when which-path information is
  *available to the environment*, whether or not anyone reads it; restored by
  genuine quantum erasure that makes it unavailable in principle.
- Status: this is standard decoherence + quantum-eraser phenomenology. Supportive.

## Tier 4 — Would break Branch A (falsifiers to keep honest)

**F1.** Any observation of super-Tsirelson correlations (`|S| > 2√2`) with
no-signalling intact would break the quantum-state reading (Branch A) as much as
it breaks QM — so it is not specific, but it is a genuine falsifier of the whole
program.

**F2.** A demonstrated, controllable faster-than-*c* influence on a knowledge
state would violate P2 and sink the theory's relativistic motivation entirely.

**F3.** If the monogamy/marginal structure (P3) were shown *incompatible* with
observed multipartite correlations (a marginal family QM allows but P3 forbids,
or vice versa), the consistency postulate would be wrong as stated.

## Summary of the testable content

- The theory's **strong, decisive** empirical commitment (Branch B) is already
  **falsified** by Bell tests. Stating this cleanly is itself a result: it tells
  us the classical-belief reading cannot be right.
- Its **viable** form (Branch A) is an interpretation; its only route to
  *distinctive* prediction is QRF frame-dependence (D2), which is the priority
  for further work.
- Everything else (D3–D5) is confirmed-but-shared with standard QM: it shows the
  picture is *consistent*, not that it is *new*.
