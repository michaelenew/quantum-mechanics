# 0001 — The Bell fork (the decisive test)

> **AI-generated, not peer-reviewed.** Prior art credited in [`ATTRIBUTION.md`](../../ATTRIBUTION.md) — results are re-derivations of
> established work unless explicitly marked otherwise.

This is the theory's most important consequence. It is not optional: any theory
built on P1 (relational beliefs) + P2 (local, *c*-bounded action) must confront
Bell's theorem, and *how* it does so determines whether it is an interpretation
or a falsified modification.

## The argument

Bell's theorem: any theory in which
1. measurement outcomes are determined by **local** variables (P2: a system acts
   only on what it knows, no faster than *c*), **and**
2. those variables encode **pre-existing shared values** (a *classical* reading
   of "knowledge state" — `W_{A→B}` is an honest probability density over real
   relative values `q_B − q_A`, updated by ordinary Bayes),

must satisfy the Bell/CHSH inequality `|S| ≤ 2`. Quantum mechanics predicts, and
loophole-free experiments confirm (Hensen, Giustina, Shalm — all 2015),
`|S|` up to `2√2 ≈ 2.83`. Therefore **premise 1 ∧ premise 2 is false.**

Our theory asserts premise 1 outright (P2). So it must reject premise 2. That is
the fork.

## The two branches

### Branch A — Interpretation (empirically equivalent to QM)
Reject the *classical* reading. The knowledge state `ρ_{A→B}` is a genuine
quantum reduced/relative state (Wigner function may be negative), and "fusion"
(P5) is the quantum state-update, not classical Bayes. "Consistency" (P3) is
read as **quantum marginal representability + agreement-upon-interaction**, not
as agreement about pre-existing shared values. Then:

- Bell violations are reproduced (the states are entangled quantum states).
- No faster-than-*c* signalling (no-signalling holds for reduced states) — P2 is
  safe.
- The price, and it is the resolving insight: **there is no frame-independent
  joint value of the two relative coordinates before interaction.** A and B agree
  *when they interact*, but the pairwise consistency of P3 cannot be the strong
  classical "they always agreed about a shared reality." It is the weak,
  quantum, perspectival agreement of RQM/QBism.

This branch is coherent and is where the idea most naturally lives. Its honest
status: an **interpretation** — it dissolves the measurement problem (collapse =
a physical coincidence event, relational) but predicts nothing standard QM does
not.

### Branch B — Modification (testable, and already in trouble)
Keep the classical reading (premise 2). Then Bell forces `|S| ≤ 2`: the theory
**predicts no super-classical Bell violation.** This is a sharp, falsifiable
prediction — and it is contradicted by four decades of experiment culminating in
the 2015 loophole-free tests. So Branch B, in its strict form, is **falsified.**

A weaker Branch-B variant survives only by adding nonlocality (giving up P2),
which contradicts the theory's own relativistic motivation, or by exploiting an
experimental loophole (detection/locality) — but the loophole-free results close
those.

## What this tells us to do

1. **Commit the theory to Branch A** as its default form, and state plainly that
   in this form it is an interpretation on the RQM/QBism axis, distinguished by
   its explicit split/merge mechanism for collapse and its variance-additivity
   consistency law.
2. **Search for daylight.** An interpretation earns "testable" status only if
   some auxiliary assumption makes it deviate. The one live candidate is
   quantum-reference-frame physics (see `0002`): if "knowledge is over relative
   coordinates in the system's own frame" is taken literally and dynamically,
   superposition/entanglement become frame-dependent in a way that is, in
   principle, measurable and that some rival readings do not share.

theory from "interpretation" toward "partial derivation of QM," which would be a
